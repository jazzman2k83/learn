AUTOSAR COM · Configuration help

Troubleshooting

Something does not behave as expected. Find what you see, then go through the checks from top to bottom. Each check names the parameter to look at.

Before you start. Most surprises come from four things: the I-PDU group is not started, a time was entered in milliseconds instead of seconds, the signal is PENDING where it should be TRIGGERED, or sender and receiver disagree on where the bits sit. Check these first.

The signal is written, but the I-PDU never goes out

  1. Is the I-PDU group started? After start-up every I-PDU group is stopped. A write to a stopped I-PDU is stored, the call returns COM_SERVICE_NOT_AVAILABLE, and nothing is sent. Look at ComIPduGroupRef of the I-PDU and at who starts that I-PDU group.
  2. Is the signal PENDING in an I-PDU without periodic transmission? ComTransferProperty = PENDING with ComTxModeMode = DIRECT means nothing ever triggers a transmission.
  3. Is the active transmission mode NONE? Either it is configured that way, or the I-PDU is on ComTxModeFalse and that says NONE.
  4. Is the signal TRIGGERED_ON_CHANGE and the value did not change? COM compares with the value sent last, or with the init value if nothing was sent yet.
  5. Is the signal a group signal? Writing a group signal only fills the shadow buffer. The whole signal group must be sent with Com_SendSignalGroup.
  6. Does the main function run? Transmissions happen in Com_MainFunctionTx. With several of them, ComIPduMainFunctionRef says which one serves the I-PDU.
  7. Does COM pass the I-PDU to the PduR, but the bus stays silent? Then the problem is below COM, in the route of the I-PDU through the PduR and the bus interface. See lesson 14.

Lesson 2, lesson 11

The I-PDU goes out later than expected

  1. The main function. A triggered transmission happens in the next Com_MainFunctionTx at the latest, not inside the write. Look at ComMainTxTimeBase.
  2. The minimum delay time. A transmission requested during the minimum delay time waits until it is over. Look at ComMinimumDelayTime.
  3. The time offset. The first periodic transmission after the I-PDU group was started waits for ComTxModeTimeOffset.
  4. Is only the transmission notification late? With ComIPduSignalProcessing = DEFERRED the notification comes in the next main function. The I-PDU itself was on time.

Lesson 3

The I-PDU goes out too often

  1. Triggered on every write. TRIGGERED triggers a transmission even when the same value is written again. TRIGGERED_ON_CHANGE does not.
  2. Repetitions. ComTxModeNumberOfRepetitions is the number of extra transmissions. 2 means three transmissions of the I-PDU per write.
  3. Periodic and triggered together. MIXED sends periodically and on every triggered write.
  4. Switching the transmission mode. A switch to the other transmission mode sends the I-PDU at once, unless that transmission mode is NONE.
  5. No minimum delay time. ComMinimumDelayTime left out or 0 means no limit on how close two transmissions can be.

Lesson 3, lesson 4

Fewer repetitions on the bus than configured

  1. A new write came in between. It cancels the repetitions that are still open and starts the count again.
  2. The signal is "without repetition". TRIGGERED_WITHOUT_REPETITION and TRIGGERED_ON_CHANGE_WITHOUT_REPETITION send once, whatever the I-PDU is configured for.
  3. A periodic transmission was counted. In MIXED, a periodic transmission that falls into the repetitions counts as one of them.
  4. The transmission timeout occurred. When ComTimeout runs out before all repetitions are done, the rest is dropped.
  5. The other transmission mode is active. ComTxModeTrue and ComTxModeFalse each have their own number of repetitions.

Lesson 3

The period on the bus is wrong

  1. Seconds, not milliseconds. ComTxModeTimePeriod = 0.1 is 100 ms. A value of 100 is 100 seconds.
  2. Which transmission mode is active? ComTxModeTrue and ComTxModeFalse can have different periods.
  3. Does the main function really run as configured? COM counts time in calls of its main function. If ComMainTxTimeBase says 10 ms but Com_MainFunctionTx is called every 20 ms, every period doubles.
  4. Is the period a multiple of the main function period? If not, it gets rounded.
  5. Is the minimum delay time longer than the period? Then the minimum delay time decides, unless ComEnableMDTForCyclicTransmission is false.

Lesson 2

The I-PDU uses the wrong one of its two transmission modes

  1. Which signals have a transmission mode condition? Every signal of the I-PDU with a ComFilter container takes part as a transmission mode condition. All others do not.
  2. One true is enough. ComTxModeTrue is used as soon as one condition is true. ComTxModeFalse needs all of them false.
  3. A condition ALWAYS. It is always true, so the I-PDU never leaves ComTxModeTrue.
  4. No condition at all. Then the transmission mode selector is always TRUE and ComTxModeFalse is never used.
  5. Right after the start. Until the SWC writes, the conditions are checked with the init values.

Lesson 4

A transmission timeout is reported although the bus looks fine

  1. Does the bus confirm transmissions? Without Com_TxConfirmation from below, transmission deadline monitoring always reports a timeout.
  2. Is the timeout shorter than the repetitions take? With repetitions, the transmission only counts as done after all of them were confirmed.
  3. Another signal's timeout. If signals of one I-PDU have different ComTimeout values, the shortest one is used for the I-PDU.
  4. Expecting it more than once? The notification is given once per failed transmission and not repeated.
  5. Was the I-PDU group stopped? Then the open transmission is reported with the error notification (COM_TX_ERR) instead.

Lesson 6, lesson 9

The I-PDU arrives, but the RTE is never notified

  1. Is the I-PDU group started? An I-PDU of a stopped I-PDU group is ignored when it arrives.
  2. Is a reception notification configured? It is configured by the user of COM, normally the RTE, as a ComUserCallback of type COM_RX_ACK, and the signal must be linked to it. In older projects: ComNotification on the signal.
  3. DEFERRED, and Com_MainFunctionRx does not run? With ComIPduSignalProcessing = DEFERRED the notification is given in Com_MainFunctionRx.
  4. An update-bit that is never set. If ComUpdateBitPosition is configured here but the sender does not set that bit, or uses another position, the signal is ignored.
  5. A filter that blocks. A value the filter blocks is discarded without a notification. Look at ComFilter of the signal.
  6. The data invalid value. With ComDataInvalidAction = NOTIFY, the data invalid value gives only the invalid notification.
  7. The I-PDU is shorter than expected. Only signals that are completely inside the received bytes are unpacked.
  8. The signal is a group signal. Notifications exist for the signal group only.

Lesson 9, lesson 5, lesson 8

The SWC always reads the init value

  1. Go through "the RTE is never notified" above. A stopped I-PDU group, an unset update-bit or a blocking filter all leave the init value in place.
  2. A group signal. The whole signal group must first be received with Com_ReceiveSignalGroup. Only then do its group signals hold new values.
  3. Read too early. With DEFERRED, a read between arrival and the next Com_MainFunctionRx still gives the old value.
  4. A timeout puts it back. ComRxDataTimeoutAction = REPLACE returns to the init value each time the timeout runs out.

Lesson 5, lesson 10

Nothing arrives, but no timeout is reported

  1. Is there a timeout? ComTimeout left out or 0 means no deadline monitoring for the signal.
  2. Did anything ever arrive? Without ComFirstTimeout, deadline monitoring only begins after the first reception.
  3. Is a timeout notification configured? A ComUserCallback of type COM_RX_TOUT, linked to the signal. Without it, only the value may change.
  4. Is the I-PDU group started? An I-PDU of a stopped I-PDU group has no deadline monitoring.
  5. Was deadline monitoring switched off? It can be switched off and on per I-PDU group while running, with Com_DisableReceptionDM and Com_EnableReceptionDM.

Lesson 6

A timeout is reported right after the start

  1. The first timeout is too short. ComFirstTimeout must cover the time the sender needs to start, plus its time offset and one period.
  2. The I-PDU group is started too early. The deadline monitoring timer starts when the I-PDU group is started. If that happens before the bus is up, time is lost.

Lesson 6

The timeout is reported again and again

  1. This is how it works. In reception deadline monitoring the timer restarts when it runs out. The notification comes after every further ComTimeout as long as nothing arrives. Only the transmission timeout is reported once.

Lesson 6

A timeout is reported although the I-PDU arrives

  1. The signal has an update-bit. Then it has its own deadline monitoring timer, and only I-PDUs with the update-bit set count. A sender that sends the I-PDU but does not write the signal causes a timeout.
  2. Another signal's timeout. Signals without update-bit share one deadline monitoring timer per I-PDU. It uses the shortest timeout of all of them, including ComFirstTimeout values.
  3. The timeout is too close to the sender's period. One late frame is then enough. Seconds, not milliseconds: 0.3 is 300 ms.
  4. The main function. COM counts time in calls of its main function. A timeout that is not a multiple of its period gets rounded.

Lesson 6, lesson 5

The value jumps back to the init value

  1. Timeout with REPLACE. ComRxDataTimeoutAction = REPLACE.
  2. Data invalid value with REPLACE. ComDataInvalidAction = REPLACE uses the init value whenever the data invalid value arrives.
  3. The I-PDU group was started again. Com_IpduGroupStart with initialisation puts all signals of the I-PDU group back to their init values.

Lesson 6, lesson 7

The "changed" filter lets an unchanged value through

  1. After a timeout. The first value after a timeout always passes MASKED_NEW_DIFFERS_MASKED_OLD.
  2. The mask. Only the bits set in ComFilterMask are compared. A change in other bits is not seen, and the other way round.
  3. Compared with what? With the last value that passed the filter, not with the last value that arrived.

Lesson 8

The receiver sees a wrong value

  1. Byte order. ComSignalEndianness must be the same on both sides. Swapped bytes are the typical sign: 0x1234 arrives as 0x3412.
  2. Bit position. ComBitPosition is the LSB of the signal for LITTLE_ENDIAN and the MSB for BIG_ENDIAN. Other tools and file formats count differently.
  3. Size. ComBitSize must match on both sides.
  4. Overlap. Two signals, or a signal and an update-bit, on the same bits.
  5. Both sides wrong in the same way? Compare each side with the network description, not with each other.

Lesson 16, signal packing sheet

A negative value arrives as a large positive one

  1. The type. ComSignalType must be a signed type (SINT8 to SINT64) on the receiving side. Only then does COM do the sign extension correctly.
  2. The size. With a wrong ComBitSize the sign bit is taken from the wrong place.

Lesson 17

Only some of the sent values reach the SWC

  1. A filter. ONE_EVERY_N passes one value in N. A range or mask filter passes only matching values.
  2. An update-bit. I-PDUs in which the sender did not write the signal are ignored for this signal.
  3. Several receptions per main function call. With DEFERRED, values that arrive faster than Com_MainFunctionRx runs can be overwritten before the RTE is notified.

Lesson 8

A forwarded signal does not appear on the other bus

  1. Does Com_MainFunctionRouteSignals run? The signal gateway copies in Com_MainFunctionRouteSignals. The I-PDUs involved point to it with ComMainFunctionRouteSignalsRef.
  2. The source has an update-bit that is not set. Then nothing is forwarded.
  3. The outgoing I-PDU is stopped. Its I-PDU group must be started too.
  4. The destination signal is PENDING and its I-PDU has no periodic transmission. The value is copied but nothing sends it.
  5. An I-PDU sent by a transport protocol. Signals of I-PDUs with ComIPduType = TP cannot be forwarded.

Lesson 12

A forwarded signal is late

  1. Three waits add up. Com_MainFunctionRouteSignals (ComMainRouteSignalsTimeBase), then Com_MainFunctionTx, then a minimum delay time of the outgoing I-PDU if one is running.
  2. The destination is PENDING. Then the value waits for the next periodic transmission of its I-PDU.

Lesson 12

The configuration tool rejects the parameters

These are the rules of the specification that are broken most easily.

  1. Update-bit together with repetitions. Not allowed in one I-PDU.
  2. A sent I-PDU without ComTxIPdu, or with neither ComTxModeTrue nor ComTxModeFalse in it.
  3. Overlapping signals in one I-PDU.
  4. ComBitSize larger than the type, for example 12 bits in a UINT8.
  5. A byte array that does not start on a byte border, or whose endianness is not OPAQUE.
  6. A filter the type does not support. FLOAT32, FLOAT64 and byte arrays: only ALWAYS, NEVER, ONE_EVERY_N. BOOLEAN: no range filters.
  7. ONE_EVERY_N with ComFilterOffset not smaller than ComFilterPeriod.
  8. A data invalid value without ComDataInvalidAction on a received signal.
  9. An I-PDU group with both directions. All I-PDUs of one I-PDU group must be sent, or all received.
  10. Names. Two of the names of signals, signal groups, group signals, I-PDUs and I-PDU groups are the same, or one is not a valid C name.
  11. A dynamic length signal that is not the last one in its I-PDU, or a second one in the same I-PDU.
  12. Forwarding between signals of different bit size.

The configurator checks most of these for a single signal.

Reading a configuration you did not write

A configuration made by a tool from the network description can be read in a fixed order. Go I-PDU by I-PDU.

  1. The I-PDU. ComIPduDirection: sent or received? ComIPduGroupRef: which I-PDU groups start it?
  2. If sent: its transmission mode. ComTxModeMode in ComTxModeTrue, and in ComTxModeFalse if there is one. Then the period, the number of repetitions and ComMinimumDelayTime. All times are in seconds.
  3. Its signals: TRIGGERED or PENDING. Does ComTransferProperty fit the transmission mode? TRIGGERED has no effect in an I-PDU that is PERIODIC. PENDING in an I-PDU that is DIRECT never sends by itself.
  4. Transmission mode conditions. Which signals of a sent I-PDU have a ComFilter? Those decide between the two transmission modes.
  5. Where the bits sit. ComBitPosition, ComBitSize, ComSignalEndianness, ComSignalType, and ComUpdateBitPosition if used. Compare with the network description.
  6. Init values. ComSignalInitValue is what a receiver reads before the first I-PDU, and what a sender puts on the bus before the first write. Is it a safe value?
  7. If received: IMMEDIATE or DEFERRED. ComIPduSignalProcessing, per I-PDU.
  8. Timeouts. ComTimeout, ComFirstTimeout, ComRxDataTimeoutAction. Remember that signals without update-bit share the shortest timeout in the I-PDU.
  9. Data invalid values and filters. ComSignalDataInvalidValue with ComDataInvalidAction, and ComFilter on received signals.
  10. Notifications. Look in the configuration of the RTE for the ComUserCallback entries linked to the signal.
  11. Main functions. ComMainRxTimeBase and ComMainTxTimeBase. Do all times above fit them, and are Com_MainFunctionRx and Com_MainFunctionTx really called that often?

To see what a set of values does, enter them in the configurator and read "What COM will do".

Behaviour and rules: AUTOSAR COM specification, R25-11, chapters 7 and 10. Only causes inside COM's own configuration are listed. An I-PDU that COM passes to the PduR correctly can still get lost in the layers below.
