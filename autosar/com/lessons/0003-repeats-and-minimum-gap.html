AUTOSAR COM

Repeats and the minimum gap

Two extras for sending. One sends an important packet several times. The other stops a packet from being sent too often.

Repeats: say it more than once

A packet can get lost on a noisy wire. For important news, such as "brake pressed", COM can send the same packet several times in a row after one write.

set per packet

Number of repeats

How many extra sends follow the first one. With 2 repeats the packet goes out 3 times.

set per packet

Repeat spacing

The time between those sends.

If the application writes a new value while repeats are still running, COM drops the remaining repeats and starts again with the new value. Old news is not worth repeating.

Minimum gap: not too often

An application might write a triggered signal a hundred times a second. Sending a packet each time would flood the bus. The minimum gap is a pause COM keeps after sending a packet before it sends that same packet again.

A send that falls inside the pause is not lost. COM holds it back and sends when the pause is over, with the newest values.

Try it

Speed is a triggered signal in a packet with no timer. Click "Write speed" a few times quickly, with different settings. Repeats are one second apart here.

Things to notice. With repeats, a second write cuts the first series short. With a gap, quick writes produce fewer packets than writes, and the packet that finally goes carries the newest speed.

The triggered family

Lesson 2 said a signal is triggered or pending. Triggered comes in four flavours. They differ in two questions: does every write count, or only a changed value? And are repeats used?

Source: AUTOSAR COM specification, R25-11, sections 7.2.3.1, 7.2.3.4 and 7.2.5.5 (SWS_Com_00330, 00767, 00734, 00768, 00279, 00305, 00698). Finer points: COM counts a repeat only once the lower layers confirm the packet was sent. A separate setting decides whether the minimum gap also applies to timer sends and repeats; in this toy it applies to every send.
