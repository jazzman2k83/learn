AUTOSAR COM

Repetitions and the minimum delay time

Two extras for TRIGGERED transmission. One sends an important I-PDU several times in a row. The other stops an I-PDU from going out too often.

Repetitions: send it more than once

An I-PDU can get lost on a noisy bus. For an important signal, such as "brake pressed", COM can send the same I-PDU several times in a row after one write. AUTOSAR calls this N-Times transmission. Two settings of the transmission mode of the I-PDU control it: the number of repetitions (how many extra transmissions follow a triggered one) and the repetition period (the time between them).

set per I-PDU

Number of repetitions

How many extra transmissions follow the first one. With 2 repetitions the I-PDU goes out 3 times.

set per I-PDU

Repetition period

The time between those transmissions.

If the SWC writes a new value while repetitions are still running, COM drops the remaining repetitions and starts again with the new value. An old value is not worth repeating.

Minimum delay time: not too often

An SWC might write a TRIGGERED signal a hundred times a second. Sending the I-PDU each time would flood the bus. The minimum delay time (MDT: the shortest time between two transmissions of the same I-PDU) prevents that. After a transmission, COM waits at least this long before it sends that I-PDU again. It works like a cool-down.

A transmission that falls inside the minimum delay time is not lost. COM holds it back and sends when the time is over, with the newest values.

Try it

Speed is a TRIGGERED signal in an I-PDU with transmission mode DIRECT. Click "Write Speed" a few times quickly, with different settings. The repetition period is one second here.

Things to notice. With repetitions, a second write cuts the first series short. With a minimum delay time, quick writes produce fewer transmissions than writes, and the I-PDU that finally goes out carries the newest speed.

The TRIGGERED family

Lesson 2 introduced the transfer property of a signal: TRIGGERED or PENDING. TRIGGERED comes in four variants. They differ in two questions: does every write count, or only a changed value? And are repetitions used?

Source: AUTOSAR COM specification, R25-11, sections 7.2.3.1, 7.2.3.4 and 7.2.5.5 (SWS_Com_00330, 00767, 00734, 00768, 00279, 00305, 00698).
