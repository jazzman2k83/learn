AUTOSAR COM

Signal types and sign extension

A signal often uses fewer bits in its I-PDU than the variable that holds it in the SWC. On reception COM widens it to fit. For negative numbers that takes one extra step: COM copies the sign bit into the new bits.

Signal types

Every signal has a signal type (ComSignalType: the data type of the signal as the RTE and the SWC see it).

Kind of valueSignal types
Yes / noBOOLEAN
Whole number, never negativeUINT8, UINT16, UINT32, UINT64
Whole number, may be negativeSINT8, SINT16, SINT32, SINT64
Number with a decimal pointFLOAT32, FLOAT64
Byte arrayUINT8_N (a byte array of fixed length) or UINT8_DYN (changing length, lesson 13)

The number in the name is the size of the variable in bits. The size in the I-PDU is a separate parameter, ComBitSize, and is often smaller. A temperature from −400 to 400 needs only 10 bits in the I-PDU, but the smallest variable that can hold it has 16.

Sign extension

When such a signal is received, COM copies its 10 bits into the 16-bit variable. Six bits on the left are left over. What goes in there?

UINT types

Fill with zeros

Zeros on the left do not change a number, just as 0042 is still 42.

SINT types

Copy the sign bit

The leftmost bit of the signal (its MSB) tells the sign: 0 is plus, 1 is minus. COM copies that bit into every left-over place.

Copying the sign bit is called sign extension (widening a signed value to the size of its signal type so that a negative value stays negative). Why is it needed? Computers store negative numbers in two's complement, which counts backwards from all ones: with 10 bits, 1111111111 is −1 and 1111111110 is −2. In 16 bits, −1 is sixteen ones. So a negative number only stays the same number if the new places on the left are also ones.

Try it

A 10-bit SINT16 signal is received and goes into a 16-bit variable.

10 bits in the I-PDU (the underlined bit is the sign bit)

16 bits for the SWC, with sign extension

16 bits if COM only filled with zeros

Things to notice. For zero and positive values both ways give the same result. For any negative value, filling with zeros gives a large wrong positive number.

Three smaller rules

Transmission needs no sign extension

On the way out, COM simply takes the lowest bits of the variable. The SWC is expected to stay within the range of the signal.

Init values are cut to size

An init value is cut to the lowest bits of the signal in the same way.

FLOAT types are left alone

For FLOAT32 and FLOAT64 COM only applies the endianness. It does no sign extension and no other conversion.

One more detail from the transmission side: the parts of an I-PDU that no signal uses are filled with a fixed byte pattern, set in ComTxIPduUnusedAreasDefault, often 0xFF.
