Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

The coordination algorithm

Lesson 1 asked how the ECUs on a bus can agree to sleep together. Network management answers it with one simple rule: an ECU that needs the bus keeps saying so on the bus, and when nobody says it any more, every ECU falls asleep at the same moment.

This lesson shows the idea behind CanNm and UdpNm, before lessons 13 and 14 add their states and timers.

Every ECU decides by itself

AUTOSAR network management is decentralized: there is no master. Every ECU of an NM cluster (the ECUs on one bus that take part in network management together) runs the same algorithm and decides only from the NM PDUs (the network management messages an ECU sends cyclically while it needs the bus) that it receives and sends.

while an ECU needs the bus

It sends NM PDUs

An ECU whose ComM has requested the network sends an NM PDU cyclically. Every ECU on the bus receives it. Receiving one means: some ECU still wants the bus awake.

each NM PDU

Restarts the timeout

Every ECU keeps a timeout, the NM-Timeout Timer (a timer that every NM PDU on the bus restarts). It restarts when the ECU receives an NM PDU and when its own NM PDU has been sent.

when no ECU needs the bus

All sleep together

An ECU that is done stops sending, but stays awake as long as NM PDUs of others arrive. When the last one stops, no NM PDU comes any more, the timeout runs out in every ECU, and after a short wait all of them go to bus sleep.

Why do they fall asleep at the same moment? Because every ECU restarts its timeout on the same NM PDU, the last one on the bus. So all the timeouts started together, and they run out together. No ECU has to tell the others "now sleep".

The same rule wakes the bus: an ECU that needs a sleeping bus starts sending NM PDUs, and the others wake up when they receive them.

Try it

Three ECUs on body CAN: the gateway (node identifier 0x10), the Door ECU (0x21) and the Light ECU (0x22). Switch their needs on and off. Each dot is an NM PDU; every dot restarts the timeout of all three. Times are scaled: one NM PDU per second per ECU, a timeout of 3 s and a wait of 1.5 s.

Body CAN: bus sleep

needs the bus bus awake timeout over: waiting

Things to notice. The ECU that needs the bus longest decides when everyone sleeps, without knowing it. An ECU that is done does not sleep on its own; it waits for the silence on the bus. The real CanNm and UdpNm add states and timers around this (for example a minimum time awake after a wakeup), but the core stays the same.
