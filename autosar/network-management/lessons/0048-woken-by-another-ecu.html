Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

Woken by another ECU

The body gateway sleeps. The Door ECU sends an NM PDU that requests PNC Comfort. The CAN transceiver wakes the gateway, EcuM validates the wakeup, ComM starts body CAN passively, and the gateway then carries the Comfort request on to Ethernet.

This flow joins the wakeup chain of Part 6 to the partial networking of Part 8. Every call, its caller and its order are taken from the R25-11 specifications; where they leave a choice to the integrator, the step says so.

The starting point

The gateway is in the SLEEP phase with a HALT sleep mode (lesson 30). All three buses are in Bus-Sleep Mode; no ComM user requests anything. The transceiver of body CAN waits in its low-power mode with selective wakeup armed: it wakes the ECU only on a wakeup frame that requests one of the gateway's PNCs (lesson 43). The driver unlocks the car, and the Door ECU requests PNC Comfort.

The example assumes two settings: synchronous wakeup is not configured in ComM (only the woken channel starts), and body CAN is an active PNC gateway channel of the gateway (lesson 44).

The five phases

wakeup · lessons 30, 33

The interrupt

The transceiver raises a wakeup interrupt. In the ISR EcuM_CheckWakeup lets the integrator's hook ask CanIf and CanTrcv, and CanTrcv reports EcuM_SetWakeupEvent. The wakeup event is pending.

validation · lessons 34, 35

Is it real?

After the WakeupRestart sequence, EcuM_MainFunction starts the wakeup source with CanSM_StartWakeupSource and asks EcuM_CheckValidation until a CAN message has arrived. CanIf_CheckValidation then calls EcuM_ValidateWakeupEvent.

passive start · lesson 36

ComM starts the channel

ComM_EcuM_WakeUpIndication marks BodyCan as requested; once BswM allows communication, ComM starts CanSM and calls Nm_PassiveStartUp. No local user needs the channel, so it goes on to COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP.

NmIf · lessons 37, 42

The coordinator and the PNC bits

Because body CAN is a coordinated channel, NmIf turns the passive startup into CanNm_NetworkRequest. The Door ECU's NM PDUs fill ERA and EIRA.

PNC gateway · lesson 44

The request goes on

The ERA bit puts PNC Comfort into COMM_PNC_REQUESTED. ComM sets the bit in its own IRA, requests full communication on BodyCan and Eth, and Ethernet wakes for the Head unit.

Not just "ready sleep until someone asks"

A plain ECU woken by the bus joins the network passively: its channel waits in COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP, its CanNm leaves the Repeat Message State for the Ready Sleep State, and the bus falls asleep again when the others stop, unless a local ComM user requests the channel first. Two features of the body gateway change that:

  • The NM coordinator. For a channel of an NM coordination cluster, NmIf treats Nm_PassiveStartUp as if ComM had called Nm_NetworkRequest, and calls CanNm_NetworkRequest. CanNm therefore goes from the Repeat Message State to the Normal Operation State and the gateway keeps sending NM PDUs until the coordinated shutdown (lesson 38), even while ComM has released the channel.
  • The PNC gateway. With the gateway function on, a PNC bit in the ERA of a gateway channel is enough to put the PNC into COMM_PNC_REQUESTED. Without it, the EIRA bit would only bring PNC Comfort to COMM_PNC_READY_SLEEP, and the channel would stay in COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP until UserComfort or UserLighting asked for it.

Within one main function ComM handles the inputs in a fixed order: requests of users mapped to PNCs, requests of users mapped to the channel, then ERA, then EIRA. So when both bits arrive together, the ERA bit decides, and the PNC goes straight to COMM_PNC_REQUESTED.

What the specification leaves to the integrator

  • The wakeup ISR that calls EcuM_CheckWakeup, the callouts EcuM_CheckWakeupHook, EcuM_StartWakeupSources and EcuM_CheckValidation, and the validation timeout.
  • The BswM rules on the wakeup state: in the example, a validated ECUM_WKSOURCE_BODYCAN leads to ComM_CommunicationAllowed for the channels. Without it the channel waits in COMM_NO_COM_REQUEST_PENDING. A rule on the expired state usually sends the ECU back to sleep.
  • The order of ComM_EcuM_WakeUpIndication and the report of the validated state to BswM inside EcuM_ValidateWakeupEvent: the requirements name both calls but no order; the figure of the specification draws ComM first.

The wakeup and validation chain is the same in 4.4.0 and R20-11, with one difference in the ISR: there EcuM_CheckWakeup was itself the integrator's callout; from R21-11 it is an EcuM function that calls the callout EcuM_CheckWakeupHook. Up to R20-11 the received PNC bits did not reach ComM through Nm_PncBitVectorRxIndication and ComM_Nm_UpdateERA: CanNm computed EIRA and ERA itself and ComM read them from COM signals (lesson 53). In 4.4.0 and R20-11 CanIf calls CanNm_RxIndication directly; the LSduR between them exists from R24-11.

Things to notice. The frame that wakes the transceiver is never seen by CanNm; it takes a second message to validate the wakeup. EcuM only reports the validated wakeup; BswM's rules allow communication, and ComM starts the channel passively. On this gateway "passive" does not stay passive for long: the NM coordinator requests the network, and the PNC gateway turns the Door ECU's Comfort request into its own request on body CAN and Ethernet.
