Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

Going to sleep

The last ComM user of the body gateway releases its request. ComM releases the network, NmIf passes the release on, the bus NM runs to Bus-Sleep Mode, and BswM sends the ECU to sleep, unless a RUN request still holds it.

This is the way back from the first two flows. Every call, its caller and its order are taken from the R25-11 specifications; where they leave a choice to the integrator, the step says so. The diagram follows chassis CAN in detail; body CAN and Ethernet ran the same way earlier, each at its own moment.

The starting point

UserComfort and UserLighting released their requests earlier, and the other ECUs no longer request PNCs, so both PNCs are back in COMM_PNC_NO_COMMUNICATION. ComM then released BodyCan and Eth, and once the Door ECU, the Light ECU and the Head unit had stopped sending NM PDUs, body CAN and Ethernet went down to Bus-Sleep Mode on their own, by the same steps as drawn here for chassis CAN. The gateway has no NM coordinator that would hold them: it uses the partial networking of NmIf, and in R25-11 an NmIf supports only one of the two (lesson 3). Only chassis CAN is still awake: ComM requests ChassisCan for UserChassis, and the Brake ECU has already stopped sending NM PDUs.

The four phases

ComM · lessons 6, 7

Release

With no request left, ChassisCan enters COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP and ComM calls Nm_NetworkRelease. The bus does not stop yet; ComM has only stopped asking for it.

NmIf · lesson 19

Passed on

The gateway has no NM coordinator, so NmIf passes the release straight on with CanNm_NetworkRelease. Only a coordinator would hold the bus for a coordinated shutdown (third variant, lesson 38).

CanNm · lessons 13, 14

Down through the NM states

Ready Sleep State until the NM-Timeout Time runs out, Prepare Bus-Sleep Mode for the Wait Bus-Sleep Time, then Bus-Sleep Mode. Each mode change reaches ComM through NmIf.

ComM, CanSM, BswM, EcuM · lessons 21, 28–30

Silent, off, asleep

ComM goes through COMM_SILENT_COMMUNICATION to COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION, CanSM switches the hardware off, and a BswM rule calls EcuM_GoDownHaltPoll once nothing needs the ECU any more.

Three buses, each on its own

Without an NM coordinator NmIf does not tie the buses together. Each bus goes down when ComM has released it and no other ECU on it sends NM PDUs any more, so the three reach Bus-Sleep Mode at different moments. The ECU sleeps only when the BswM rule sees all channels in COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION. Body CAN differs in one detail: it uses partial networking, so CanSM leaves the transceiver with selective wakeup armed (lesson 43), ready for the next flow of lesson 48.

A gateway configured as an NM coordinator (third variant) holds the released buses instead until every bus of its cluster is ready to sleep, then releases each after its shutdown timer: with the shutdown times of lesson 38 (global coordinator time 4 s) Eth at once, BodyCan after 0.5 s and ChassisCan after 1.5 s, so that all three reach Bus-Sleep Mode at about the same time.

A request in the middle stops all this. If a ComM user asks for a channel before its bus sleeps, ComM calls Nm_NetworkRequest, NmIf passes it on, and CanNm keeps or takes up sending NM PDUs (lesson 13). A bus that already sleeps is woken actively, as in lesson 47. A coordinator would also abort a coordinated shutdown that has started (lesson 39).

What the specification leaves to the integrator

  • The BswM rule that decides on sleep. A typical rule waits until all channels are in COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION and EcuM reports that the last RUN request is released; what it checks is configuration.
  • Its action list: stopping the I-PDU groups (already on the silent or no communication mode), ComM_CommunicationAllowed with FALSE, EcuM_SelectShutdownTarget with the target SLEEP and a sleep mode, then EcuM_GoDownHaltPoll. The specification only requires that a sleep target is selected before EcuM_GoDownHaltPoll.
  • Whether BswM evaluates the rules at once or in its main function, and the callout EcuM_EnableWakeupSources that arms the wakeup sources of the chosen sleep mode.

The flow is the same in 4.4.0 and R20-11: the CanNm states, the ComM transitions and EcuM_GoDownHaltPoll (new in 4.4.0). There NmIf had no partial networking of its own (it ran in CanNm and UdpNm), so the example gateway could also be an NM coordinator and shut the three buses down together, as in the third variant. One detail of the coordinator changed in R25-11: up to R24-11 a shutdown timer is started for every awake channel of the cluster, since R25-11 for every awake, actively coordinated one; for the third variant, whose channels are all actively coordinated, that makes no difference. Up to 4.3.1 BswM started the sleep with EcuM_GoHalt or EcuM_GoPoll instead; with EcuM Fixed, EcuM itself decided when to leave RUN (lesson 51).

Things to notice. Releasing the network is not switching it off: after Nm_NetworkRelease the gateway may stay awake for seconds, held by the other ECUs and the NM timers (and, in the third variant, by the coordinator). The way down is driven by indications from below (Prepare Bus-Sleep Mode, then Bus-Sleep Mode), each answered by ComM with a new request to CanSM. Buses asleep and ECU asleep are two decisions: the buses sleep by NM, the ECU sleeps only when a BswM rule says so.
