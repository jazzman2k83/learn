Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

NmIf: between ComM and the bus NMs

ComM talks to one module about network management, whatever the bus: NmIf. NmIf passes each request down to CanNm or UdpNm, and each change of their mode back up to ComM.

The lessons before looked at CanNm on its own. Above it sits NmIf, one instance for all channels of the ECU. This lesson shows its base functionality: the part that every ECU with NM needs.

A layer that hides the bus

NmIf is a bus-independent adaptation layer. It converts the generic calls from ComM into calls of the bus-specific NM of that channel, and the callbacks of the bus-specific NMs into generic callbacks to ComM. ComM never calls CanNm or UdpNm itself; other modules are meant to reach network management only through NmIf too.

Each NM channel of NmIf refers to one ComM channel and has a bus type. On the example ECU, network handle 0 (BodyCan) belongs to CanNm and network handle 2 (Eth) to UdpNm. The same network handle is used on every layer, so Nm_NetworkRequest(0) becomes CanNm_NetworkRequest(0).

The calls of the base functionality

For its base functionality NmIf only forwards. ComM uses three requests, and four callbacks come back:

CallDirectionMeaning
Nm_NetworkRequestComM → NmIf → CanNm/UdpNmThis ECU needs the bus: NM goes to (or stays in) Network Mode and sends NM PDUs.
Nm_NetworkReleaseComM → NmIf → CanNm/UdpNmThis ECU no longer needs the bus: NM stops sending and sleeps with the others.
Nm_PassiveStartUpComM → NmIf → CanNm/UdpNmStart Network Mode from Bus-Sleep or Prepare Bus-Sleep Mode without requesting the network, for example after a wakeup by another ECU.
Nm_NetworkStartIndicationCanNm/UdpNm → NmIf → ComMAn NM PDU arrived in Bus-Sleep Mode: other nodes are already in Network Mode. NM does not wake up on its own; ComM decides.
Nm_NetworkModeCanNm/UdpNm → NmIf → ComMNM has entered Network Mode.
Nm_PrepareBusSleepModeCanNm/UdpNm → NmIf → ComMNM has entered Prepare Bus-Sleep Mode.
Nm_BusSleepModeCanNm/UdpNm → NmIf → ComMNM has entered Bus-Sleep Mode; ComM switches the channel to COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION.

NmIf forwards each callback under the ComM name: Nm_BusSleepMode becomes ComM_Nm_BusSleepMode, and likewise ComM_Nm_NetworkStartIndication, ComM_Nm_NetworkMode and ComM_Nm_PrepareBusSleepMode. The callbacks of NmIf may run in task or in interrupt context. On a channel in passive mode NmIf refuses Nm_NetworkRequest and Nm_NetworkRelease with E_NOT_OK. For a bus without NM PDUs of its own (a local NM, for example on LIN) NmIf answers a request of ComM itself with ComM_Nm_NetworkMode.

Try it

Step through the calls for body CAN, then switch to Ethernet: the calls of ComM do not change, only the module below NmIf does. Then follow the other start, when another ECU wakes the bus.

More than forwarding

NmIf also passes on the optional calls of the earlier lessons. ComM does not use them; they exist for extensions of the NM stack, and NmIf only forwards them when the bus NM supports them:

  • Down: Nm_DisableCommunication and Nm_EnableCommunication (called by BswM, lesson 17), user data, PDU data, Nm_RepeatMessageRequest and node identifiers (lessons 15 and 16), the remote sleep check and the NM state.
  • Up: Nm_CarWakeUpIndication goes to a callout or to BswM, Nm_StateChangeNotification goes to BswM (lesson 18). Nm_RemoteSleepIndication stays inside NmIf.

Two parts of NmIf do real work of their own. The NM coordinator (a gateway function in NmIf that keeps several buses awake together and shuts them down together) uses the bus NMs to bring several networks to sleep at the same moment; part 7 of the course explains it. And for partial networking NmIf receives the PNC bit vectors of all channels, filters and aggregates them, and reports them to ComM (part 8).

In 4.4.0 and R20-11 NmIf took no part in partial networking: CanNm and UdpNm evaluated the PNC bits themselves, and ComM read the result through COM signals (part 10). The PNC bit vector callbacks of NmIf appear in R21-11 as a draft. The base functionality of this lesson is the same in 4.4.0, R20-11 and R25-11.

Things to notice. NmIf adds no state of its own in the base functionality: every request and every callback goes straight through, with the same network handle. That is why ComM is the same for body CAN and Ethernet. A received NM PDU in Bus-Sleep Mode does not start NM; it is reported up, and ComM answers with Nm_PassiveStartUp.
