Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

What network and mode management is for

A parked car must use almost no current, yet any ECU may need to talk at any moment. The buses must wake up together, stay awake while anyone still needs them, and fall asleep together.

This lesson shows the problem that the whole course is about, and the three questions an ECU (electronic control unit: one control unit with its microcontroller and software) has to answer about its communication.

A night in a parked car

A vehicle has dozens of ECUs. Most of them are connected to a bus (a communication medium several ECUs share, such as a CAN bus or an Ethernet network). While the car is parked, the battery has to last for weeks, so almost every ECU and every bus has to be switched off. A bus in which no ECU communicates any more and whose hardware is in a low-power mode is in bus sleep.

Then the driver pulls the door handle. The door ECU needs to tell the light ECU to switch on the interior light, so the body bus must come back. That event is a wakeup (whatever ends a sleep: a message on the bus, a pin, a timer). After the driver has left, each ECU finishes its work at a different moment. The bus may only go to sleep once the last one is done.

Two easy answers do not work. If every bus simply stayed awake, the battery would be empty after a few days. If every ECU went to sleep on its own whenever it was done, the others would lose their partner in the middle of a conversation and report communication errors. The ECUs have to agree.

Three questions, three kinds of module

Each ECU has to answer three questions all the time. In the AUTOSAR BSW (basic software: the standardized modules below the RTE) a different kind of module answers each one.

inside this ECU

Does anyone here need the bus?

ComM (Communication Manager) collects the requests for communication inside the ECU and switches each bus on or off for this ECU. An SWC (software component: application software above the RTE) asks it through the RTE (Runtime Environment, which connects the SWCs to the basic software).

between the ECUs

May the bus sleep?

Network management (the coordination between the ECUs of one bus about when it may sleep) runs on every ECU. While an ECU needs the bus it keeps announcing that on the bus; when nobody announces it any more, all of them sleep together.

the ECU itself

Should the ECU run, sleep or switch off?

Mode management (deciding which mode the ECU and its communication are in, and switching the modules accordingly) is done by EcuM (ECU State Manager: startup, wakeup, shutdown and sleep) and BswM (BSW Mode Manager: configured rules that decide what happens when).

The rest of the course takes these modules one at a time. Lesson 2 places all of them in the layers of the basic software, and lesson 4 follows one request through all of them.

Things to notice. The bus does not sleep when one ECU is done, but when the last one is, and then only after a short wait. Requesting communication inside one ECU (ComM) and agreeing with the other ECUs (network management) are two different jobs, done by different modules.
