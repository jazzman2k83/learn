Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

Coordinating several buses

The body gateway sits on three buses. Its NM coordinator keeps all of them awake as long as anyone needs any one of them, and only then lets them go to sleep together.

Up to now every bus decided on its own when it may sleep. This lesson shows what changes when an ECU on several buses ties them together, and where in NmIf that happens.

One gateway, three buses

The example gateway is on body CAN (BodyCan), chassis CAN (ChassisCan) and Ethernet (Eth). Each bus has its own network management: CanNm on the two CAN buses, UdpNm on Ethernet. Each of them only knows its own bus. Body CAN would fall asleep as soon as the Door ECU, the Light ECU and the gateway are all ready, no matter whether the Brake ECU is still talking on chassis CAN.

For a gateway that passes data from one bus to another this is often not what the vehicle wants. The usual wish is: while any ECU on any of these buses still needs its bus, all of them stay awake; when nobody needs any of them any more, all of them go to sleep. NmIf offers exactly this as its NM coordinator functionality.

Where the coordinator works

The NM coordinator is an optional part of NmIf; when it is not configured, NmIf only passes calls through between ComM and the bus NMs. When it is configured, the buses it ties together form an NM coordination cluster (a set of buses that the coordinator keeps awake together and shuts down together). The rules:

  • An ECU may have several coordination clusters; each one is coordinated independently.
  • Each bus belongs to at most one cluster. A bus in no cluster is not coordinated: NmIf forwards ComM's requests for it unchanged.
  • A bus in a cluster is a coordinated channel (a channel that the NM coordinator keeps awake and shuts down together with the other channels of its cluster). Its bus NM must be able to send NM PDUs, so a bus NM in passive mode cannot be coordinated.
  • Several coordinators may sit on the same bus, for example two gateways in a chain. Lesson 39 shows how they agree.
  • A bus whose sleep this ECU alone decides, such as a LIN bus where the ECU is the master, can be configured as a sleep master: the coordinator treats it as ready to sleep at all times.

In the example all three channels, BodyCan, ChassisCan and Eth, form one cluster.

Keeping coordinated buses alive

All AUTOSAR bus NMs share one principle: an ECU keeps its bus awake as long as it keeps the network requested. The coordinator uses that principle. It keeps the network requested on every awake bus of the cluster (through CanNm_NetworkRequest and UdpNm_NetworkRequest) as long as one of two things is true:

need inside the gateway

This ECU needs one of the buses

ComM has requested the network on at least one bus of the cluster with Nm_NetworkRequest. Example: UserChassis needs ChassisCan, so body CAN and Ethernet stay awake too.

need on a bus

Another ECU needs one of the buses

At least one bus of the cluster is not ready to sleep because another ECU still requests it. Example: the Door ECU keeps body CAN awake, so the gateway keeps chassis CAN and Ethernet awake, even if it needs none of them itself.

The specification sums it up in one sentence: as long as at least one node, the gateway included, keeps any bus of the cluster awake, the coordinator keeps all buses of the cluster awake.

How does the coordinator know that the other ECUs on a bus are ready? From the bus NM. While the gateway keeps sending NM PDUs, CanNm and UdpNm watch whether any other node still sends. When no other node has sent for a while, the bus NM reports a Remote Sleep Indication to NmIf; when one starts sending again, it reports a remote sleep cancellation. The coordinator only needs these two notifications per bus, plus ComM's requests and releases.

What the coordinator does not do

The coordinator only keeps awake buses awake. A bus of the cluster that is already in Bus-Sleep Mode is watched, but the coordinator does not wake it up. Waking buses is the job of ComM: when a bus wakes up, ComM decides whether the other buses of the cluster are started as well. Lesson 39 comes back to this.

The other half of the job, letting the buses go to sleep together once nobody needs any of them, is the coordinated shutdown (the coordinator releases the buses one by one with calculated delays so that they reach Bus-Sleep Mode at the same moment). It is the topic of the next lesson.

Things to notice. The coordinator is not a new kind of NM message; it is NmIf calling the network request of each bus NM, exactly as ComM does for a single bus. One need anywhere in the cluster, inside the gateway or on any bus, keeps every awake bus of the cluster requested. A bus outside the cluster lives its own life.
