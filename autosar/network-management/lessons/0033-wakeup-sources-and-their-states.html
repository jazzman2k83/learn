Wakeup sources and their states

Everything that can start or wake the ECU, a CAN transceiver, a pin or a reset, is one wakeup source for EcuM, with one bit of its own. Each wakeup source moves through four states, and EcuM reports every change to BswM.

This lesson shows how EcuM names its wakeup sources, how a driver reports a wakeup, and what the four states mean. Lesson 34 follows the validation step by step, lesson 35 a whole CAN wakeup, lesson 36 what ComM and NM make of it.

One bit per wakeup source

A wakeup source (anything that EcuM knows as a reason why the ECU started or woke up: a bus, a pin, a timer, a reset) is one bit of the type EcuM_WakeupSourceType (a 32-bit bit field with one bit per wakeup source). EcuM manages up to 32 of them. Its functions take and return such bit fields, so one call can name several sources at once.

Five bit positions are predefined, for the reasons why the microcontroller starts: power cycle, hardware reset, internal reset, internal watchdog and external watchdog. All other wakeup sources are configured; the configuration tool gives each one a bit from 5 to 31. A configured wakeup source can also name the ComM channels it belongs to, and the PNCs.

Wakeup events

A driver that sees its hardware wake up, such as the CanTrcv after activity on body CAN, reports a wakeup event (the report that a wakeup source has woken up) to EcuM with EcuM_SetWakeupEvent (the EcuM function a driver calls to report a wakeup; the source becomes pending or validated). The call ORs the given bits into EcuM's set of pending events. It may be called from an interrupt, from a task, and even before the OS runs, because drivers also report wakeups found during their initialization. Bits of a source that the selected sleep mode does not allow are ignored.

A driver reports a wakeup event once, and does not report it again until it has been armed again. Other modules read the result with EcuM_GetPendingWakeupEvents, EcuM_GetValidatedWakeupEvents and EcuM_GetExpiredWakeupEvents.

Not every report is real: a spike on the CAN wires or a bouncing contact can look like a wakeup. Such a source is configured with wakeup validation (a check that the wakeup was real, for example that a CAN message follows, within a configured time). A source configured without it counts as validated as soon as it is reported.

The four states

EcuM keeps one state per wakeup source and reports it to BswM as a mode request through BswM_EcuM_CurrentWakeup (EcuM's report of the new state of one or more wakeup sources to BswM). The states are the values of EcuM_WakeupStatusType (the state of a wakeup source: none, pending, validated or expired):

ECUM_WKSTATUS_NONE

None

No wakeup event was detected, or it has been cleared with EcuM_ClearWakeupEvent (clears the given sources from the pending, validated and expired events).

ECUM_WKSTATUS_PENDING

Pending

Pending (reported, not yet validated): EcuM starts the source for validation and runs the validation timeout (how long EcuM waits for the validation).

ECUM_WKSTATUS_VALIDATED

Validated

Validated (the wakeup is confirmed): a source with channels is passed to ComM with ComM_EcuM_WakeUpIndication (EcuM tells ComM that a channel has woken up).

ECUM_WKSTATUS_EXPIRED

Expired

Expired (not validated in time): EcuM stops the source again.

EcuM handles the wakeup sources all the time in its main function, in parallel to everything else, not only after a sleep. It does not decide what the ECU does about a state: BswM rules do. When a source is validated, a rule brings the ECU back to normal operation; when every source is back to none or expired, a rule prepares the sleep again and calls EcuM_GoDownHaltPoll.

    Reset reasons are wakeup sources too

    The one type also says why the ECU started. During startup EcuM asks the MCU driver for the reset reason (why the microcontroller started: power on, a reset, a watchdog) and maps it, by configuration, to a wakeup source. It remembers that source, so the main function handles it like any other wakeup event. If EcuM cannot tell the reset reason, it sets ECUM_WKSOURCE_RESET.

    The five predefined sources are never validated: a power cycle or a watchdog reset cannot be a spike on a wire. That is why ECUM_WKSOURCE_POWER in the simulation goes straight to validated.

    Some transceivers switch the power of the whole microcontroller and switch it on again on bus activity. For such hardware a wakeup on CAN looks like a power-on reset: the ECU runs through STARTUP, and the integrator code must ask the transceiver driver whether it caused the start, report the wakeup event, and validate it as usual. The CAN transceiver specification counts this case as a cold start, not as a wakeup.

    The four states, the predefined bits and the functions of this lesson are the same in 4.4.0 and R20-11. Two differences around them: those releases have EcuM_EndCheckWakeup, with which a driver that checks its source asynchronously reports that no wakeup was found (removed in R21-11), and the EcuM specification describes no Ethernet wakeup sequence yet (added in R21-11).

    Things to notice. A wakeup source is just a bit, and one call can carry several bits. Whether a source is validated is a matter of configuration: without validation, reporting it already makes it validated. EcuM only reports the states; what the ECU does about them is decided by BswM rules.
