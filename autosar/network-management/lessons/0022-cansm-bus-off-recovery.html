Network Management

CanSM: bus-off recovery

After too many transmission errors a CAN controller switches itself off the bus. CanSM restarts it, first quickly, then more slowly if the errors keep coming, and tells ComM, BswM and the diagnostics what happened.

What a bus-off is

Every CAN controller counts its transmission errors. When the count gets too high, the controller enters bus-off (a controller state after too many transmission errors in which it disconnects itself from the bus). A broken wire, a short circuit or a wrong baud rate are typical causes. The controller then neither sends nor acknowledges frames, so a faulty node cannot disturb the others for ever.

The Can driver reports the event to CanIf, and CanIf passes it on to CanSM with CanSM_ControllerBusOff. CanIf also switches the PDU mode of that controller to transmit offline at once: from now on it refuses transmit requests from above and clears its transmit buffers, while reception stays possible.

The rest is bus-off recovery (CanSM restarts the controller after a bus-off, first after a short time, level 1, later after a long time, level 2, and reports the event to DEM). It runs inside the full communication state of lesson 21, in its own small sub state machine.

The recovery cycle

On the bus-off, CanSM first tells BswM (CANSM_BSWM_BUS_OFF), then ComM: the channel is now in COMM_SILENT_COMMUNICATION. It reports the event to DEM (Diagnostic Event Manager: stores diagnostic events such as a bus-off) as pre-failed. If the network has several controllers, CanSM stops the others too. Then it restarts the controller and waits until the controller reports that it has started.

The controller is running again, but CanSM keeps the transmit path closed for a while: the recovery time. Only after that time, counted from the bus-off, does it switch CanIf back to online, report full communication to BswM and then COMM_FULL_COMMUNICATION to ComM. Frames, including the NM PDU, go out again.

Level 1, level 2 and the counter

short wait

Level 1

For the first bus-offs in a row, CanSM waits only the short level 1 time before sending again. A single disturbance costs very little.

long wait

Level 2

CanSM counts the bus-offs without a successful recovery in between. Once that count reaches a configured threshold, it waits the long level 2 time instead, so a node with a permanent fault blocks the bus only now and then. Whether the bus-off that has just happened already counts, the specification does not say; the simulation below counts it.

reset

The check

After sending is switched on again, CanSM checks whether the recovery worked. When the check passes, DEM gets passed and the count of bus-offs in a row starts again from zero.

The check can work in two ways, chosen in the configuration. Either CanSM simply waits a configured time after switching transmission on; if no new bus-off came in that time, the recovery counts as successful. The time must be long enough that frames are really sent in it, for example longer than the period of the fastest cyclic I-PDU. Or CanSM asks CanIf with CanIf_GetTxConfirmationState whether a transmit confirmation has arrived since the restart, for every controller of the network, and passes the check as soon as one has.

Instead of the two fixed times, a network can also get its wait time from a callout function after each bus-off.

ComM's own state does not change: its users still request full communication, and the mode it wants for the channel stays full communication. Only the mode the bus actually has, as indicated by CanSM, is silent for a while; ComM passes that indicated mode on to its users and to BswM. A bus-off does not end communication, it pauses sending.

CanSM reports to DEM at every bus-off and at every successful check. DEM decides through its debouncing how many pre-failed reports in a row make a stored fault; CanSM itself stores nothing.

A bus-off can also happen while the channel is in silent communication (for example while the network is going to sleep). CanSM then only restarts the controller and reports pre-failed to DEM; sending stays off, as silent communication requires.

Try it

Press Start, then Inject bus-off. Switch on "Fault stays" to see what a permanent fault does: every time sending starts again, the next bus-off follows. In this example level 1 waits 1 s, level 2 waits 4 s, level 2 starts with the third bus-off in a row, and the time check lasts 2 s. Times are scaled.
