Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

NM variants per channel

Not every bus has network management, and not every ECU may keep a bus awake. Each channel therefore says how ComM works with network management on it, and that decides what happens after the last request is released.

Lesson 7 ended in COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP: no ComM user needs the channel any more, and ComM waits until the channel may go down. What it waits for is set per channel by its NM variant (the way a channel is handled with respect to network management: FULL, PASSIVE, LIGHT, NONE, SLAVE_ACTIVE or SLAVE_PASSIVE).

Two variants with AUTOSAR NM

NM keeps the bus awake

FULL

With FULL, AUTOSAR network management (CanNm, UdpNm) runs on the channel. When a user requests full communication, ComM requests the network from NmIf; on entering COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP it releases it. While the network is requested, NM sends NM PDUs (the network management messages an ECU sends cyclically while it needs the bus) and so keeps the bus awake. NM then decides when the bus may sleep: ComM goes to COMM_SILENT_COMMUNICATION when NM prepares bus sleep and to COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION at bus sleep.

NM follows the others

PASSIVE

With PASSIVE, AUTOSAR NM runs, but in passive mode (the NM of this ECU never sends NM PDUs itself). ComM never requests the network: a request takes the channel to full communication and at once on to COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP. The ECU is not allowed to keep the bus awake; it stays awake while the other ECUs do and goes down with them, by the same NM indications as FULL.

Only these two variants give a synchronized shutdown: all ECUs on the bus go to sleep at the same moment, because network management decides it for all of them.

Four variants without AUTOSAR NM

On the other four variants ComM calls no NM service at all. Something else ends full communication.

VariantUsed forKeeps the bus awake?What takes the channel down
LIGHTA bus without NMOnly while requested; there is no agreement with the other ECUsA timeout in ComM: after the last release the channel stays in COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP for a configured time, then goes straight to COMM_NO_COMMUNICATION
NONEA bus without NM, and every internal channelYes, once requestedNothing in ComM: the channel stays in COMM_FULL_COM_READY_SLEEP until the power of the ECU is switched off
SLAVE_ACTIVEA slave on a bus with a master, for example a LIN slaveNo, but its request can make the master wake the busThe master: ComM goes to no communication when the bus state manager reports bus sleep
SLAVE_PASSIVEA slave that always follows its master, for example an Ethernet channel whose hardware complies with OA TC10No; no ComM user and no PNC may request this channelThe master, as for SLAVE_ACTIVE

On LIGHT and NONE channels a request also keeps the channel in COMM_FULL_COM_NETWORK_REQUESTED for a configured minimum time, so that the channel does not toggle. FULL needs no such timer: NM already keeps a woken bus awake for a minimum time. LIGHT cannot synchronize the shutdown, so an ECU may wake up again and again because a message arrives from an ECU that goes to sleep later.

On the body gateway

All three channels of the example ECU run AUTOSAR NM: CanNm on BodyCan and ChassisCan, UdpNm on Eth. The gateway must be able to keep each of these buses awake for its own users, so all three channels are FULL. A door module that only reacts to the others could be PASSIVE; a LIN slave behind a LIN master would be SLAVE_ACTIVE.

In 4.4.0 the variants are FULL, LIGHT, LINSLAVE, NONE and PASSIVE. LINSLAVE is the LIN slave case and behaves like SLAVE_ACTIVE today. R20-11 replaces it with SLAVE_ACTIVE and adds SLAVE_PASSIVE for Ethernet slaves; R20-11 already has the six variants of R25-11.

Things to notice. Only FULL lets this ECU keep the bus awake through NM; with PASSIVE the ECU is on the bus only while the others keep it awake. Only FULL and PASSIVE shut the bus down together with the other ECUs. LIGHT skips COMM_SILENT_COMMUNICATION, and NONE never leaves full communication by itself.
