Network and Mode Management in AUTOSAR Classic

Network Management

The modules and their layers

Network and mode management is not one module but a dozen. Each sits in a fixed layer of the BSW and talks only to a few neighbours: the SWC and the RTE on top, ComM, BswM and EcuM in the middle, the buses at the bottom.

This lesson is a map. It names every module of the course, puts each one in its layer and shows who talks to whom. The details of each module follow in their own parts of the course.

Three kinds of job, three groups of module

The AUTOSAR layered architecture puts the basic software in layers: the Services Layer at the top, below it the ECU Abstraction Layer, and at the bottom the Microcontroller Abstraction Layer with the drivers. The modules of this course fall into three groups.

System Services

ComM, BswM, EcuM

The managers. ComM collects the requests for communication, BswM runs the configured rules that switch the other modules, EcuM starts the ECU, handles wakeups and shuts it down.

Communication Services

NmIf, CanNm, UdpNm, CanSM, EthSM

The workers per bus. The NmIf (Network Management Interface: passes the requests of ComM to the NM of each bus) dispatches to CanNm (CAN Network Management: sends and receives the NM messages on one CAN network) or UdpNm (UDP Network Management: the same for Ethernet). A bus state manager (the module that switches the hardware of one bus for ComM) exists per bus type: CanSM (CAN State Manager) and EthSM (Ethernet State Manager).

ECU Abstraction and drivers

CanIf, EthIf and the drivers

CanIf (CAN Interface: hands frames and mode requests to the CAN driver and transceiver) and EthIf (Ethernet Interface: the same for Ethernet) hide the hardware. Below them the Can driver (controls the CAN controller in the microcontroller), the CanTrcv (CAN Transceiver Driver: the chip between the controller and the bus wires), the Eth driver (controls the Ethernet controller) and the EthTrcv (Ethernet Transceiver Driver: the PHY chip at the cable).

Around them sit the modules that carry the application data, known from the COM course: COM (puts signals into I-PDUs), the PduR (PDU Router: forwards each I-PDU, a block of data such as a group of signals, to the right bus interface), and on Ethernet SoAd (Socket Adaptor: maps PDUs to sockets) and TcpIp (the TCP/IP stack). Dcm (Diagnostic Communication Manager: answers a tester's diagnostic requests) also lives in the Communication Services. Network and mode management does not send this data itself, but it decides when it may flow: BswM starts and stops each I-PDU group (a set of I-PDUs that COM starts and stops together).

Who talks to whom

Follow a request for communication through the map; lesson 4 does it call by call.

  • The SWC asks ComM through a port of the RTE. It never calls the bus modules.
  • ComM passes the request down two paths per bus: to the bus state manager (CanSM or EthSM), which switches the hardware on, and to the NmIf, which starts network management on that bus through CanNm or UdpNm. Both report back to ComM when their part is done.
  • CanSM switches the transceiver and the controller through CanIf. EthSM does the same through EthIf and also brings TcpIp online.
  • CanNm sends its NM messages through the LSduR (L-SDU Router: hands PDUs between the upper modules and CanIf) and CanIf. UdpNm sends them as UDP messages through SoAd and TcpIp.
  • BswM hears the modes from ComM and the bus state managers, and by its rules sets communication allowed in ComM and starts or stops the I-PDU groups in COM.
  • EcuM receives the wakeups from CanIf and the transceivers and passes a validated wakeup to ComM and BswM.
  • Dcm tells ComM while diagnostics is active on a channel, and ComM tells Dcm which mode the channel is in.

The LSduR between CanIf and the modules above it is new. Up to R20-11 (and in 4.4.0) CanNm and the PduR call CanIf_Transmit directly; the CanNm specification uses the L-SDU Router from R24-11, the CanIf specification from R25-11. The control path of CanSM to CanIf is unchanged.

Things to notice. ComM never touches the hardware: it hands every request to a bus state manager and to NmIf. NmIf is one module for all buses; CanNm, CanSM and CanIf exist once per bus type and handle each network of that type. The modules that carry application data (COM, PduR) are not part of network management, but BswM switches them.
