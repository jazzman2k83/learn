SOME/IP in AUTOSAR Classic

SOME/IP

The modules on the data path

In the Classic Platform no single module "is" SOME/IP. The work is shared by a chain of modules between the application software and the Ethernet wire.

The map

These colours stay the same in every diagram of the course: green is always SoAd, pink is always Sd, and so on.

Three groups of jobs

making the message

Top: RTE and transformer

The RTE (Runtime Environment: connects the SWCs to each other and to the basic software) takes the data of the SWC (software component: the application) and lets the SOME/IP transformer (the module that serializes the data into a SOME/IP message) turn it into bytes and write most of the SOME/IP header. COM (the communication module) or LdCom (Large Data COM, its lean alternative) then carries the bytes as one signal.

getting it to the right place

Middle: PduR, SoAd, Sd

The PduR (PDU Router) forwards each PDU (Protocol Data Unit: a block of data handed between modules, known by its PDU ID). SoAd (Socket Adaptor) maps PDUs to socket connections and adds the rest of the header. Sd (Service Discovery: the module that offers, finds and subscribes services) also decides which routes in SoAd are switched on.

moving bytes

Bottom: TcpIp, EthIf, Eth

TcpIp (the TCP/IP stack) does UDP, TCP and IP. EthIf (Ethernet Interface) hands frames to the right protocol. The Eth driver (Ethernet driver) moves frames in and out of the hardware.

Two side paths matter for SOME/IP. Messages too big for one UDP datagram take a detour through SOME/IP TP (SOME/IP Transport Protocol), which does the segmentation (cutting the message into segments that each fit into one UDP datagram; the receiver reassembles them). And SOME/IP-SD messages (the messages of Service Discovery) do not come from an SWC at all: Sd builds them itself and hands them straight to SoAd. BswM (BSW Mode Manager) never touches a message; it tells Sd which services are wanted.

Not shown: EthSM, ComM and the Ethernet transceiver driver, which bring the network up and down. The course assumes the link is up.

Things to notice. The SOME/IP header is written by two modules: the SOME/IP transformer writes most of it, SoAd adds the Message ID and the Length. Sd and BswM never touch the data of an event or a method call; they only decide whether it may flow.
